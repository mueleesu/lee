Tracing “Moon Jar” Across Museum Sources

TypeSource tracing
QuestionTerminology
LanguagesKorean / English
OutputEvidence trail
A miniature demonstration of a cultural-history task: recover what current institutional sources actually say, distinguish repetition from independent evidence, and identify the primary-source gap.

Prompt

A client encounters the claim that the painter Kim Whanki is connected to the modern Korean name dal hangari (“moon jar”). What can an initial source pass establish?

Source trail

National Museum of Korea curator material states that these large round white porcelain jars were not nicknamed “moon jars” until the 1950s. A separate National Museum exhibition text links the name to Kim Whanki. The Metropolitan Museum of Art uses “Moon jar” as a present-day catalogue title.

Next-stage archival work

  • Search digitized Korean newspapers, exhibition catalogues, artist writings, and criticism from the 1950s onward for early appearances of 달항아리.
  • Record exact wording, publication date, author, page, and context rather than saving only modern summaries.
  • Distinguish “coined the term,” “used the term,” and “popularized the term.”
  • Build a chronology showing the earliest verified usage found and leave unresolved gaps unresolved.
A useful research handoff is sometimes an evidence trail rather than a confident sentence. This sample intentionally stops before claiming a primary-source origin for the name.
