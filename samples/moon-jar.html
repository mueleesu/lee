Demonstration sample prepared from public museum records. It shows research structure and source handling rather than authentication, valuation, or a complete catalogue entry.

Question

What can a researcher establish quickly and reliably about the Korean object type commonly called the “moon jar,” and how can comparable museum records frame a first-stage object inquiry?

Finding

The National Museum of Korea identifies moon jars as large round white-porcelain vessels of the Joseon dynasty and explains that the form was made by joining upper and lower hemispherical sections. Its curator material places the appearance of the type from the late seventeenth century into the mid-eighteenth century. The Metropolitan Museum of Art catalogues a comparable example to the second half of the eighteenth century.

Research practice: object chronology, terminology, collection provenance, and later reception history are separate evidentiary questions.

Research leads

  • Compare construction, profile, glaze, seam, foot, and measurements across documented examples.
  • Separate the history of the ceramic form from the twentieth-century history of the name.
  • For a client-owned object, record condition, repairs, marks, labels, and ownership documentation before drawing comparisons.
  • Keep authentication, valuation, and legal ownership questions outside a preliminary research brief.
