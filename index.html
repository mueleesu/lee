Joseon Moon Jar

Question

What can a researcher establish quickly and reliably about the Korean object type commonly called the “moon jar,” and how can comparable museum records frame a first-stage object inquiry?

Finding

The National Museum of Korea identifies moon jars as large round white-porcelain vessels of the Joseon dynasty and explains that the form was made by joining upper and lower hemispherical sections. Its curator material places the appearance of the type from the late seventeenth century into the mid-eighteenth century. The Metropolitan Museum of Art catalogues a comparable example to the second half of the eighteenth century.

Research practice: object chronology, terminology, collection provenance, and later reception history are separate evidentiary questions.

Tracing “Moon Jar” Across Museum Sources

National Museum of Korea curator material states that these large round white porcelain jars were not nicknamed “moon jars” until the 1950s. A separate National Museum exhibition text links the name to Kim Whanki. The Metropolitan Museum of Art uses “Moon jar” as a present-day catalogue title.

SourceRecovered claimCaution
NMK Curator's PicksNickname dates to the 1950sInstitutional secondary source
NMK exhibition textKim Whanki linked to namingNeeds earlier documentary evidence

Next-stage work

  • Search digitized Korean newspapers, exhibition catalogues, artist writings, and criticism from the 1950s onward for early appearances of 달항아리.
  • Distinguish “coined the term,” “used the term,” and “popularized the term.”
  • Leave unresolved documentary gaps unresolved rather than filling them by inference.
A useful research handoff is sometimes an evidence trail rather than a confident sentence.

Korean Ceramics Record Normalization

Collection information often arrives in mixed formats: inconsistent titles, dimension strings, institution-specific classifications, compound accession numbers, and partial notes. A working sheet needs a predictable schema while keeping every source reversible.

Schema: Record ID · Institution · Canonical Title · Culture/Dynasty · Date/Period · Material/Technique · Dimensions · Accession No. · Location/Department · Source URL · Research Note
